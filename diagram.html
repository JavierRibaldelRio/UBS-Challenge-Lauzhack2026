How the Financial Twin works,
from your words to a verified answer

A fictional person, their real (synthetic) bank history, and a twin you can talk to. You describe a decision in plain words; the twin turns it into a plan, simulates 500 possible futures, answers your question with a confidence it can justify, and checks every number it tells you.

1 The big picture

Top row happens once, before the demo. The middle and bottom rows happen every time you type something.

  1. You type in the chat: a decision (“what if I do a master’s?”), a fact about today (“I now earn 1,500”), or a change to an existing path (“make the flat 500k”).
  2. AI #1, the twin’s voice (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash on Together AI, reasoning off), gets the context: the last 12 messages, your current numbers and results, and every path on screen. In one strict-JSON turn it replies and acts: updates facts about today, adds new paths, edits the path you mean (“have the kid at 35 instead”), or removes one it got wrong. Any amount you didn’t say stays empty. It describes; it never calculates the simulation.
  3. The resolver builds a month-by-month plan and fills every empty number from the data (e.g. “median salary of 69 employed people aged 20–24”), labelling each one: from data learned you assumption unknown.
  4. The engine runs exact bookkeeping, month by month, 500 times with realistic luck learned from the data, and returns ranges, risks and goal chances.
  5. AI #2 receives only the final numbers and writes, for the two paths you compare, what happened so far (from the real data) and what tends to happen next, plus a personal comparison; and the direct answer to a question. The number checker verifies every number it wrote. You see “✓ all 13 numbers checked”.

1b The screen: a tree of possible lives, two at a time

🌳 A scenario tree, as in UBS’s examples

Every path is a node. A branch keeps every decision of its parent and adds one (work → then a flat → then a child). Placement shows what builds on what, not when: the time is on the links (“+8 yrs”), and you can click it to change it. Pan, zoom, drag nodes; a node’s “+” opens a card next to it.

🟢 A vs 🔵 B

Click two nodes: the first is A (green), the second B (blue). Tiles say in plain words what happened so far (real data) and what tends to happen next; a comparison measures liquidity, net worth, goals and constraint breaches (UBS’s four catalog metrics) and names the path that fits your goals better, in this simulation, by a rule shown on screen. Labelled Not financial advice.

📈 Year by year + sensitivity

Net worth and cash in the bank, A and B, with the real bank balance as black dots. The sensitivity card shows what moves the result most and what happens if an assumption is off by up to 30%, and where the order of A and B would flip.

2 Three layers: what is certain, what is predicted, what is assumed

CERTAIN

⚙️ Bookkeeping formulas

New cash = old cash + income − spending − costs − loan payments. Net worth = cash + investments + home − debts. The same for everyone, exact, and checked live on every change.

PREDICTED · TESTED

🎲 Learned luck

How much spending jumps around, which months are expensive, how often a bonus comes. This is the only part that predicts, and it predicts uncertainty, not outcomes. Trained on 2025, it matched what really happened in 2026 (§11).

ASSUMED · VISIBLE

🧭 Your decisions

“Do a master’s”, “buy a flat” are applied as explicit changes to the plan. Nobody in the data does a master’s, so no model could honestly predict its effect. Instead every number is labelled, editable, and unknowns are shown as unknown (§9).

2b Two kinds of variables: the person and the economy

UBS defines a twin as xt+1 = F(xt, at, wt; θ) and asks it to “distinguish user-controlled decisions from external conditions”.

IDIOSYNCRATIC · per person, per path

👤 The person

Income, rent, usual spending (estimated from 28,800 months: status, city, calendar month), their decisions (study, job, move, home, kid, loan…) and their personal luck (surprise expenses, December bonus). Each path is one set of decisions.

MACROECONOMIC · shared by every path

🌍 The economy

Inflation vs pay, unemployment, interest rates, market returns, recessions, house prices. One setting for every path, so decisions are compared fairly. “Forecast (Swiss data)” draws inflation and unemployment for each future from a model of Swiss data 1991–2025 (employees can lose their job and get Swiss unemployment benefit). Stress-test presets follow UBS’s scenarios (S101 inflation, S103 rate hike, S104 recession, S108 housing, S110 crisis) plus an “uncertain economy” with random recessions.

what if I do a master’s and there’s a recession in 2 years?
＋ Master’s degree (a path: idiosyncratic) · 🌍 Economy for every path: recession at 22 (macroeconomic)

In the Monte Carlo, each simulated future draws personal luck (estimated from data) and, in the forecast or uncertain economy, economy luck: a recession or a job loss in future #17 hits every path in future #17.

4 What the models estimate (in R)

📅 Status × month

+373

CHF extra spending (besides rent) for employed people in February; +357 in August. One value per job status and calendar month, from a mixed model chosen among four by BIC (R/eda.R).

🏙️ City

−121 … +176

CHF/month vs the average city, from Fribourg to Zurich. Moving city moves spending by the difference. Students’ tuition-like Feb/Sep peaks are left out when a degree pays tuition explicitly.

🎁 December bonus

55%

of employed people get one in a given year; typical size 60% of a month’s pay (self-employed: 27%).

🎲 Surprises

your spread

A bell curve around each person’s own month-to-month spread (pulled toward people like them), wider in Feb, Aug and Dec. No personal habit that lasts: ±CHF 16/month.

🌍 Economy forecast

VAR(1)

Swiss inflation and unemployment 1991–2025 (World Bank, R/macro.R). Tested 2005–2024: 1 year ahead the 80% range held 70% (inflation) and 95% (unemployment) of the time.

👥 Peer ranges

10th–90th %

E.g. employed people aged 20–24 earn CHF 4,963 to 10,519 (median 8,070, 69 people). Used to test how robust an answer is.

5 Inside one simulated month

income = planned net income + maybe a December bonus 🎲 (forecast economy: maybe a job loss → benefit 🎲)
spending = rent + usual spending for your status, city and month ± surprise 🎲 (× prices vs pay in the forecast economy)
cash = cash + income + new loans − spending − extra costs − one-off costs − loan payments − money moved to investments − down payment
investments = (investments + money moved in) × (1 + return) × (1 − market shock)
debt = debt + interest − payment (never below 0)
net worth = cash + investments + assets (home, car…) − debt
green money inred money outblue moves between your own pockets: changes cash, not net worthpurple luck (estimated)

Example: buying a 600k flat with 20% own money. Cash −120k, debt +480k (mortgage), assets +600k: net worth stays flat, the money just changed form. A car works the same way, then loses value every year. The engine also checks, in every future, that the 120k is actually there.

6 500 possible futures, the same luck for every path

Illustration: thin lines = single futures; bold = the middle one (median); shaded = where 8 in 10 land.

Each path is simulated 500 times. Each run draws different surprise months and bonuses from what the model learned.

Future #1 of “master’s” and future #1 of “start working” get exactly the same luck, so when two paths differ, it’s because of the decision, not chance.

From the 500 runs come: the line (median), the shaded range, “cash runs out in X%”, and “goal reached in X%”. A fixed seed means the same result on every laptop.

Monte Carlo is a tool here, not the product: it handles month-to-month luck. Uncertainty about the assumptions is handled separately (§8, §9).

It sees the last 12 messages, your current numbers and every path, so “instead”, “that path”, “the kid” make sense, and corrections (“no, I meant rent”) fix the right thing. 12 event types (including buying a car or other assets); “A or B” → separate paths; other currencies are never converted silently. 54 live tests, including two real messy conversations replayed turn by turn, French and very long sentences.

✦ AI #2: explaining back, for A and B

So far In UBS’s data your bank balance went from CHF 8k to −17k in two years… Ahead In this simulation you reach about CHF 710k at 30 and cash stays above zero…
On cash, Start working is clearly calmer… In this simulation, Start working fits your goals better: the master’s runs out of cash in 100% of futures. More money is not automatically the better fit. Not financial advice.

Gets only the final numbers (and the verdicts the app computed). Rules: plain, warm words; use only these numbers; never pick a different “better fit” than the computed one; never “you should”. The app checks every number (understands “526k”, “12’000”, “35%”, French spacing). Typical speed: 1–3 seconds.

8 Answering your question, with a confidence that is computed, not guessed

Master’s in Zurich, or start working at 22 for 6,000 a month?
In this simulation, the Master’s in Zurich ends higher: about CHF 430k vs CHF 375k… Low confidence

The level comes from the engine, by the type of question:

⚖️ “A or B?”

1. Luck: in how many of the 500 paired futures does A end higher? 2. Peers: does A still win if its salary is at the low end of real peers and B’s at the high end? 3. Unknowns: does it depend on a number nobody knows, and how much would have to change to flip it?

🎯 “Can I reach my goal?”

The share of futures that reach it. 0% or 100% is clear-cut (high); around 50% is a coin flip (low).

🏠 “Can I afford it?”

The share of futures where cash never goes below zero and the down payment is really there at that moment.

High = luck ≥ 95% and holds against peers and no unknown that matters · Medium = luck ≥ 80% and (holds or needs ≥ 20% to flip) · Low = otherwise. The AI must copy the level exactly; if it doesn’t, the app shows a warning.

9 Showing what nobody knows

Example: the master’s salary premium. The data has no education field, so nobody knows it. The tag says unknown, and the twin assumes “no change” (0%).

On the chart, the path turns dotted from the month that number starts to matter (the end of the degree), a ? marks it, and a hatched band shows how far it could go (illustrative range, clearly labelled).

Two ways to make it solid: type the number into “The numbers behind”, or say it (“the degree raises my salary by 20%”). And instead of guessing, the twin shows the break-even: “the master’s only catches up if it raises the salary by 23%.”

11 Why you can trust the numbers

The R model was estimated on 2025 only; the app’s own simulator predicted 2026 for all 1,200 people, and we compared with what really happened in the data.

“X% of results fall in this range”

we said really happened

“A ends higher than B in X% of futures”

we said really happened (19,980 pairs of people)

✓ Accounting, live

Cash adds up every month; moving money between pockets never changes net worth; debt never negative; same seed → same result. Checked on every change.

✓ Better than a simple average

Typical miss CHF 1,831 vs 2,445; cash-crunch prediction score 0.0006 vs 0.0025 (lower is better).

✓ Tests

95 automatic tests (UBS’s validation list: zero income, higher tuition, loan payoff…; the tree; the comparison; parity with the R model; the economy forecast; the backtest) + 67 tests against the real AI, including real conversations replayed turn by turn. All pass.

12 Built to never break during a demo

🔌 AI down?

Keyword rules understand questions, a template writes the story, ready-made paths never need AI. The maths never depends on the AI.

📶 Wi-Fi down?

Every successful AI answer is cached in the browser and replayed for the same question.

🧾 Bad AI output?

Strict JSON schema, validation, one automatic repair attempt, then the fallback. Numbers in text are always checked.
